 Q: Explain what the term “full coverage” means with respect to 
 tests, and why it may or may not be a reasonable goal for 
 some projects.

Full coverage means having a test for every line of code you 
write, testing all possible ways to use a function and 
each branch in a loop. It can also help ensure your code stands 
up against different types of input. There are a few issues with 
full coverage, and why it may not be a reasonable goal for your 
program. First, it's not always possible to know and test every 
scenario your code may go through. This brings Sims 4 to mind. 
where there are multiple different types of DLC that can 
significantly change the game while also interacting 
with other DLC packs. Every person's game looks very  
different. Because of the complexity, it can be difficult to test 
every single scenario for each line of code. Full coverage shouldn't 
be thought of as a "catch all" when working with big, complex programs. 

Bringing me to my second point, depending on the size of the program it 
may not be worth it to get full coverage. Achieving full coverage for a 
massive program, like Sims 4, would require a lot of time and energy that 
may not be readily available. Especially for a program still in development 
or taking on additions. For situations like this, it may be better to make 
sure all critical pieces of code are tested as thoroughly as possible and 
look at full coverage once the program is in a finished state (beyond 
regular updates) and if widely used.
